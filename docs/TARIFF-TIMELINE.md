# Tariff timeline: U.S.-Canada measures on vehicles, parts, steel, aluminum and broad goods

Coverage: January 1, 2025 to November 2, 2026. Compiled November 2, 2026.

Scope: U.S. and Canadian tariff measures that affect U.S.-Canada trade in vehicles,
auto parts, steel, aluminum, and broad tariffs on Canadian or U.S. goods. Each row
cites a primary source. "USMCA" is the U.S. name for the agreement; Canada calls it
"CUSMA." Times are 12:01 a.m. Eastern unless the source says otherwise. "Effective"
means goods entered for consumption on or after that date. Federal Register links use
the `federalregister.gov/d/<document number>` form.

Stacking rule that applies to many rows: Executive Order 14289 (signed April 29, 2025,
retroactive to entries on or after March 4, 2025) says goods subject to the Section 232
auto tariff are not also charged the IEEPA Canada tariff or the Section 232 steel and
aluminum tariffs, and goods subject to the IEEPA Canada tariff are not also charged the
Section 232 steel and aluminum tariffs. Source: [90 FR 18907](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-07835).

## Checks on the reported items

- Temporary 10% global tariff: confirmed. Section 122, Proclamation 11012, Feb 24 to Jul 24, 2026. USMCA goods and Section 232 goods were exempt.
- Section 301 measure effective Jul 24, 2026: confirmed. This is the forced labor action. Canada's rate is 10%. USMCA duty-free goods and Section 232 goods are exempt.
- Section 338 change around Oct 30, 2026: confirmed. Proclamation 11076, signed Oct 23, effective Oct 30, 2026. It changes the product list; the rate stays at 50%.
- September 2026 increase to 50%: confirmed for Canada. On Sep 8, 2026, Canada raised most of its counter-tariffs on U.S. steel and aluminum from 25% to 50% (SOR/2026-187). No U.S. Section 232 rate change took effect in September 2026.
- September 1, 2025 removal: the Canadian order removed counter-tariffs on everything on the 2025-1 list, plus non-steel and non-aluminum goods on the steel order. It was not limited to CUSMA-compliant goods. Steel, aluminum, and vehicle counter-tariffs stayed.

## Could not confirm

1. **Section 122 rate increase to 15%.** Secondary sources report a 15% rate was announced Feb 21, 2026. No Federal Register document amends Proclamation 11012 (API search on "Proclamation 11012" returns only the proclamation and a CBP de minimis rule). The best reading is that the rate stayed at 10%, but no primary source says so directly.
2. **USMCA auto parts content process.** Proclamation 10908 exempts USMCA-qualifying parts until Commerce publishes a process to tax non-U.S. content. No such notice turned up in Federal Register searches through Nov 2, 2026. Commerce's parts inclusion windows (Dec 2025, Mar 2026) add parts to the tariff list; whether any inclusions took effect was not checked.
3. **Section 338 annexes.** The annexes in the Federal Register are images. Product details here come from CBP's HTS lists (Aug 21 and Oct 29, 2026). The Sep 15, 2026 list (CSMS 69851916) was not read, so it is not clear whether the steel, aluminum, and HS 8703 lines in the Oct 30 list were added on Sep 15 or Oct 30. CBP's Oct message says that from Sep 15, 2026 only dairy-list goods (heading 9903.03.13) may still claim the Section 232 exemption (heading 9903.03.15). The annex of the Sep 29 ban (Proc. 11063) was not reviewed.
4. **Size of Canada's September 2026 counter-tariffs.** Finance Canada says C$27.6 billion. The White House fact sheet of Sep 8, 2026 says "about $20 billion of U.S. exports." The difference may be currency (Canadian vs. U.S. dollars) or scope; neither source explains it. Finance Canada also gives C$27.6 billion as the value of Canadian goods hit by the U.S. Section 338 duties.
5. **Section 338 USMCA treatment.** The White House fact sheets say the duties apply regardless of USMCA origin. The proclamation text available as text does not say this directly; the rule may be in the image annexes.
6. **Interim expiry dates of Canada's remission order.** The original six-month relief ran to Oct 16, 2025 (stated in the SOR/2025-181 analysis). Justice Laws lists amendments on Jun 26, 2025, Oct 15, 2025, Dec 11, 2025, Feb 5, 2026, Jun 22, 2026, and Sep 8, 2026. A Finance Canada search result said relief was extended to Jan 31, 2026 for steel goods and to Jun 30, 2026 for auto and aerospace steel and aluminum; the Dec 12, 2025 release confirms the Jun 30, 2026 date for auto and aerospace steel. The step-by-step dates for each amendment were not checked one by one. Only the current text (through Jun 30, 2027) is in the table.
7. **Status of Canada's auto remission framework after April 2026.** A Finance briefing (via search result) said it would run one year from Apr 9, 2025. Finance opened consultations on changes on Feb 27, 2026. Current status was not found. The remission order's motor vehicle section (s. 4.3) has no time limit.
8. **IEEPA refunds in court.** Secondary sources say the Court of International Trade ordered refunds (Atmus Filtration v. United States, Mar 4, 2026), that the government appealed, and that CAPE Phase 3 started Oct 6, 2026. The refund totals they report differ ($134.7 billion as of Sep 17; $136.6 billion as of Oct 2). None of these were checked against court records or a CBP message. CBP's refund page does not discuss appeals.
9. **Section 232 metals rate cut for Canadian suppliers.** Proclamation 10984 lets Commerce lower steel and aluminum rates for USMCA metal from Canada or Mexico. No Commerce notice granting such a cut was found.
10. **USMCA annual review steps after July 2026.** A USTR Federal Register notice dated Oct 2, 2026 (posted on ustr.gov) on the next annual review was found in search results but not read.
