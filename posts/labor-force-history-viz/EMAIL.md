## 3. Body, part one

```
In March we published a map of every U.S. county's labor force against the same month 20 years earlier. It answers one question at a glance: is this county's labor force growing or shrinking over the long run?

The first version was built in Tableau, and it had limits. You could not watch the map move. Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico sat on a separate view. And the trend chart for each county started at zero, which flattened the very changes we wanted you to see. So we rebuilt it from the ground up, straight from the Bureau of Labor Statistics' county data. The new map answers a second question too: is that changing now?
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
Here is what is new.

Press play. Watch the map move one month at a time from January 2010 to the latest month, at 1x, 2x or 5x speed.

One map. Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico now sit on the same map as the lower 48.

A fair trend chart. Hover over or tap any county to see the 20 years behind its color. Every county uses the same vertical scale, so a 10% drop looks the same everywhere, and no county is stretched to look worse or better than it is.

Pick a state. Zoom to any state or territory, and every count on the page follows.

Watch flags. Stripes mark counties whose labor force is turning. Rising stripes mean it has climbed off a bottom and is still climbing. Falling stripes mean it is still sliding. A county can be in structural loss and still be turning up, and now you can see it on the map.

The visualization is free. The page also walks through every step of how we built it, so you can check our work or rebuild it yourself.
```
