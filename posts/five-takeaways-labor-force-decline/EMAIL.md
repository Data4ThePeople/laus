## 3. Body, part one

```
We rebuilt our county labor force map this week and added data through July 2026. Then we went looking for what changed.

A county is in structural loss when its labor force is more than 10% below where it stood 20 years earlier. In 2010 that was 7% of counties. It is now 40%, or 1,281 of 3,214, the highest reading on record. At the end of last year, the data behind our first map, it was 33%.

The reason it moved so fast is in the next chart.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
Counties normally add workers over the first seven months of the year. This year 38% of them lost workers instead. The long-run figure is 21%, and the only worse year on record is 2020.

Whole states are moving together. In Michigan 72 of 83 counties are on negative watch and not one is recovering. The three Detroit metro counties are holding, and almost everything outside them is not.

South Carolina is the mirror image. No county in the state is on negative watch, and the counties turning up hold 95% of its workers.

The part that surprised us most is what happened to the boom counties. Two years ago, 3% of the hyper-growth counties were shrinking. Today 48% are, and that list includes Denver, Wake County in North Carolina, and Loudoun County in Virginia.

Five charts, plus the map itself, are in the post.
```
