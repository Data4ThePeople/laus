# X thread: Five takeaways from our new labor force map

Post: https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/five-takeaways-labor-force-decline
Map: https://data4thepeople.github.io/laus/

Attach the named image to each post. Counts below treat a link as 23 characters, the way X does.

---

**1/**
```
40% of U.S. counties now have a labor force more than 10% smaller than it was 20 years ago.

1,281 of 3,214 counties. The highest reading on record.

We rebuilt our county labor force map and added data through July 2026. Five things stood out.
```
Image: `images/01-structural-loss-over-time.png`

---

**2/**
```
At the end of last year it was 33%.

What moved it: counties normally add workers over the first seven months of the year. This year 38% of them lost workers instead.

The long-run figure is 21%. The only worse year on record is 2020.
```
Image: `images/02-january-to-july.png`

---

**3/**
```
Whole states are moving together now.

In Michigan, 72 of 83 counties are on negative watch, meaning their labor force is below both a year ago and three years ago. Not one county in the state is recovering.

The three Detroit metro counties are holding. Little else is.
```
Images: `images/03a-negative-watch-states.png`, `images/03b-mi-negative-watch.png`

---

**4/**
```
Colorado is the opposite shape. There the decline starts at the top.

All ten of its largest counties are shrinking, Denver included. Only Boulder is on negative watch so far, because that flag also asks for a three-year decline.
```
Image: `images/03c-co-largest-counties.png`

---

**5/**
```
It is not all one direction.

In South Carolina, 39 of 46 counties are turning back up and none is on negative watch. The counties gaining hold 95% of the state's workers.

We looked for a reason and found migration, not a factory boom.
```
Images: `images/04a-positive-watch-states.png`, `images/04b-sc-positive-watch.png`

---

**6/**
```
The one that surprised us most.

Hyper-growth counties are those whose labor force is more than 40% above its level 20 years ago.

Two years ago, 3% of them were shrinking.

Today 48% are.
```
Image: `images/05a-hypergrowth-falling.png`

---

**7/**
```
That list includes places you would not expect. Wake County, NC peaked in July 2025. Denver peaked in September 2024. Loudoun County, VA peaked in July 2024.

A few are still climbing: Benton County, AR, Charleston, SC and Midland, TX.
```
Images: `images/05-1-wake-nc.png`, `images/05-3-denver-co.png`, `images/05-4-loudoun-va.png`, `images/05-6-benton-ar.png`
