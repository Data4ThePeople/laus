```
40% of U.S. counties now have a labor force more than 10% smaller than it was 20 years ago.

That is 1,281 of 3,214 counties, and it is the highest reading on record. At the end of last year it was 33%.

We rebuilt our county labor force map this week and added data through July 2026. Five things stood out.

1. Two in five counties are shrinking. The share in structural loss went from 7% of counties in 2010 to 40% today.

2. This year is the weakest on record outside 2020. Counties normally add workers over the first seven months of the year. This year 38% of them lost workers instead, against a long-run figure of 21%.

3. Whole states are moving together. In Michigan, 72 of 83 counties are on negative watch, meaning their labor force is below both a year ago and three years ago, and not one county in the state is recovering. Colorado is the opposite shape: there the decline starts at the top, with all ten of its largest counties shrinking, Denver included.

4. A few states are going the other way. In South Carolina, 39 of 46 counties are turning back up and none is on negative watch. The counties gaining hold 95% of the state's workers. We went looking for a reason and found migration and rising participation, not a factory boom.

5. Even the boom counties have stalled. Among counties whose labor force is more than 40% above its level 20 years ago, 3% were shrinking two years ago. Today 48% are. That list includes Wake County, North Carolina, which peaked in July 2025, Denver, which peaked in September 2024, and Loudoun County, Virginia, which peaked in July 2024.

The labor force counts people who are working or looking for work, where they live. It falls when people retire, stop looking, or move away, so a shrinking labor force is not the same thing as rising unemployment.

All five charts, the method, and the map are in today's Data 4 Thought. The map is free: press play to watch 16 years, pick your state, and look up your own county.

https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/five-takeaways-labor-force-decline

Data: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Local Area Unemployment Statistics.
```
