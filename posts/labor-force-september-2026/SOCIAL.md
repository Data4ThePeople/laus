```
Michigan's labor force data has a problem, and Michigan is not the only state.

In September, 20 Michigan counties had entered structural labor force loss over the past year, and none had left. That is half of the national net increase of 40 counties. Structural loss means a labor force more than 10% below where it stood 20 years earlier.

Initially, our September update was going to be about Michigan. But as we worked through our validation steps, something concerning arose. Michigan's data in one BLS labor database disagrees with its data in another.

If we asked a group of Michiganders, "how many of you are employed," and a group of employers, "how many employed people do you have," the answers should be at least similar, right? They are not. The employer data says jobs are flat in Michigan. The household survey data says employed residents are down 4.5% since January 2025.

So we went looking through other states' data. In September, the one-year change in employed residents trailed the change in jobs by 2 points or more in 14 states. In June it was 18, as many as at the 2021 high.

COVID was a shock to our labor force and to the surveys that measure it, and BLS tried to make changes in real time. Something like that may be happening now. Not knowing which measure is correct may be even scarier than knowing.

Nationally, 1,132 of 3,214 U.S. counties were in structural loss in September, 35.2%. The full update, the charts and our county map are at the link in the first comment.

#LaborForce #Michigan #PublicData #DataJournalism
```
